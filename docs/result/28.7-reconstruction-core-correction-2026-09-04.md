## 1. Executive Summary

This wave was asked to close known generic reconstruction defects *before* new public websites are
tested, so that a fresh-URL test would be meaningful rather than a re-run of known failures.

**What it did.** It closed both P0s — a preparation scroll can no longer destroy a route, and neither
can a slow screenshot. It made the top-ranked defect (A9, frozen 1440-px values shipping at every
width) *measurable* for the first time, with a per-node audit that names the offending node, its
frozen property, its measured error in pixels and its descendant count. It closed A12 (one site-wide
tree-switch breakpoint against routes that disagree) with a DOM-family fingerprint. It taught grid
recovery spans and hidden children. It added four QA honesty guards with numeric coverage
conservation. It ran the first authoritative full regression since Task 28.5B and found two breaks
nobody had seen, one of them dating from 28.6.

**What it did not do: move fidelity.** The final canary is 24 pairs at **10 BLOCKER / 7 MAJOR /
7 MINOR / 0 PASS**. An independent visual auditor, grading 20 pairs from the images alone, put the
engine at roughly **75% human visual equivalence** against a ~95% target — bimodal: at or above 95%
on static content at a width the source was designed for, 55–70% everywhere else.

**The honest one-line summary is that this wave measured the disease and did not cure it.** Its own
instrument now says so precisely: `width` is 91.3% of the residual on linear.app and 99.1% on
hobbang.net, `grid-template-columns` is 5.7% and 0.3% — and this wave's largest recovery work was on
grid tracks. Two independent auditors said the same thing in different words, and both are right.

**Verdict, from a fresh independent adjudicator who was given both permitted strings and no
indication of which was wanted (§27):**

# NOT READY - CORE BLOCKERS REMAIN

**Two defect classes stand between the engine and the bar, and they are independent:**
1. **The frozen desktop width (A9).** At 700 / 1024 / 1100 the clone lays out at a fixed ~1390px and
   the right of the page is sliced off — hero headlines chopped mid-word, whole footer columns and an
   entire pricing column unreachable.
2. **Content that only exists after the page's own JavaScript runs.** Hero sliders, news feeds, promo
   carousels and scroll-revealed text blocks are reconstructed as *empty containers*. A blank white
   band where a hospital's hero photograph belongs; a clinic's entire doctor biography absent.
   **No QA channel fires on "this region should have content and is empty."**

Class 2 was not in this wave's defect ledger at all. It was found by looking at the pictures.

## 4. Normal Page State

New module `src/observer/normalize-page-state.ts` runs after `networkidle` + fonts and **before**
collection. The observer's header contract was rewritten to say so explicitly: the observer may now
perform bounded, evidence-gated normalization before collection; **collection itself stays
read-only**. This was decided deliberately rather than drifted into, and is recorded as such in
`docs/result/28.7/01-normal-page-state.md`.

`src/observer/observe-page.ts` also gained a deterministic settle (A3): the page is done when document
height and renderable-image count both hold still across a required run of samples. `networkidle`
became **one bounded input**, not the gate. Every cap is recorded, so a page that never settles is
visible *as* a page that never settled. The denominator for image stability is renderable candidates,
not `document.images` — a page declaring 6 `<img>` where only 4 can render counts 4.

Tests: an endless-poll fixture that provably never reaches `networkidle` and settles anyway; a
slow-image fixture; a negative control for every positive.

## 5. Popup Handling

**The predicate is deliberately narrow: modal-SHAPED *and* ≥2 signals *and* ≥1 STRONG.**

| strength | signals |
|---|---|
| STRONG | `declared-dialog`, `appeared-after-initial-paint`, `close-control-inside` |
| weak | `page-scroll-locked`, `stacked-above-page` |

Two weak signals alone are never enough. At most 2 dismissal attempts, `force` is never used, and
`Escape` is sent only with `declared-dialog` evidence.

**There is no DOM-removal path in the module at all** — verified independently by the architecture
auditor, not merely asserted here. The design fear was a false positive deleting a real fixed header;
the answer was to make deletion unrepresentable rather than to make the predicate cleverer. When
dismissal fails, that is recorded and the census describes the page as it is.

Framework dismissal attributes (`data-bs-dismiss` and friends) are deliberately **not** read — that
would be a per-framework hack in a system that must work on arbitrary sites.

### Two failures of this feature that the wave shipped without noticing

**1. It does not fire at the desktop viewport, on the very site used to demonstrate it.** Measured from
`data/seoultone.kr/site-observations/2026-09-04T18-13-50-161Z/pages/p000001/observation.json`:

| viewport | `scans` | `structuralMatches` | `qualified` | `dismissed` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **desktop (1440)** | 5 | **0** | **0** | **0** |
| mobile (390) | 2 | 1 | 1 | **1** (`section#popup_slider`) |

The 1440 clone ships the entry popup baked in — exactly the defect A2 exists to prevent — while the 390
clone does not.

**The cause is generic, which makes it worse than a seoultone quirk.** The modal-SHAPED gate reuses
`OVERLAY_SHAPE` (`src/observer/types.ts:1629-1636`), whose thresholds are **viewport-relative**:
`minWidthCoverage: 0.5`, `minHeightCoverage: 0.5`, `minAreaCoverage: 0.5`. A popup with a fixed pixel
width — this one is ~31% of 1440 — covers well over half of a 390px viewport and well under half of a
1440px one. **The same modal qualifies at narrow widths and is invisible to the normalizer at wide
ones, on any site**, and the wide viewport is where entry popups are most common.

Nothing asserted normalization symmetry across viewports on a real site, so nothing caught it.
**A2 is demonstrated at mobile and undemonstrated at desktop.**

**2. The two capture paths have different page-state policies and nothing reconciles them.**
`normalizePageState` has **zero callers under `src/responsive-qa/`**. The observer removes an entry
popup; the QA source capture does not. The clone is then charged `missing-text` for a popup the engine
deliberately removed — on seoultone `/`@390, part of the `missing-text-ratio 0.3054` BLOCKER is exactly
this. This is a bias **introduced by this wave**, and it points the wrong way: it makes the clone look
worse than it is.

Both are carried to 28.8.

## 6. PrepareScroll Navigation Safety

The 28.6 mechanism, restated: the preparation scroll caused a page to navigate mid-observation, which
destroyed the JS execution context, which filed the page `observation-error`, which cost the route its
`renderSourcePageId`, which made `pnpm reconstruct` refuse outright. Losing the page entirely was the
worst available outcome and it was what happened.

**Implemented.** `autoScrollPrepare` was split into `runScrollPass` plus a non-throwing recovery
wrapper (`src/observer/layout-probe.ts:392-566`). A new `src/observer/navigation-errors.ts` carries the
destroyed-context signatures and `isSameObservedDocument`. Status vocabulary:

`prepare-scroll-complete` | `prepare-scroll-navigated-recovered` | `prepare-scroll-navigated-fallback` | `prepare-scroll-failed-fallback`

**The subtle half is the per-step document identity check** (`layout-probe.ts:296-343`). A navigation
that *completes* during the 250 ms step settle throws nothing — the context is not destroyed, it is a
different document, and the scroll silently continues down the wrong page. The URL is therefore checked
every step. One bounded recovery plus one retry; a final non-retrying restore `goto` is recorded
separately as `restoreNavigated`.

**A1b — the second door, found by rejecting a green report.** The observer work package reported
218/218. Two fresh orchestrator runs failed non-deterministically in *different* sections. Sent back
for root cause rather than retried, it proved to be `page.screenshot({ fullPage: true })` carrying no
explicit `timeout` and silently inheriting Playwright's 30 s default action timeout —
`page.setDefaultNavigationTimeout()` moves only the navigation budget. On overrun it threw out of
`observeViewport` and the page was filed `observation-error` with no artifact: **the exact P0 this task
exists to remove, through a second door, and it would fire on any slow real site.** Now an explicit
60 s budget with a 20 s fallback, a recorded `screenshotDegraded` field, and a negative control proving
the field means something.

**Result:** seoultone.kr observes all 13 pages, reconstructs, and grades. The 28.6 blocker is closed.

## 10. QA Honesty Corrections

Four guards, each verified by mutation, each paired with a coverage assertion so a falling count cannot
be read as improvement:

| guard | what it stops |
|---|---|
| clone-route-missing | a route the clone never built (HTTP 404) graded as content loss |
| source-capture-unstable | a source that swings 4× at one width and reverts, graded anyway |
| duplicate-image-stacking | a crossfade's two layers read as a layout collapse |
| measurement-failure-explicit | a failed capture that is never silently green |

New `CoverageAccounting` type with `conserved`, asserted numerically:
`pairsGraded + pairsCloneRouteMissing + pairsSourceCaptureUnstable + pairsMeasurementFailed = pairsTotal`.
`coverage.conserved` is `true` on all four final canary runs.

The overlap sweep was moved out of the in-page probe into a pure `src/responsive-qa/overlap.ts` so it
can be unit-tested against synthetic geometry.

### Three things about this work package that independent review found, and that must be said

**L1 — the `failed-image-layer` branch is checked first and demotes on *either* leaf failing to load.**
The `duplicate-image-stack` branch is genuinely tight (both leaves images, same key *and* `ownerKey`,
every edge within 2px, key-alone explicitly rejected). This one is not. A real collapse where a
correctly-loaded element lands on a broken image is demoted out of BLOCKER-capable
`overlap-excess-ratio` into `image-layer-state`, banded MAJOR at most. `overlap-excess-ratio-undemoted`
exists for exactly this case and **no channel fires on it**, so the mitigation is inert.

**L2 — none of the four guards fired on the real corpus.** All demotion counters are **0** on all four
final runs. The guards are fixture-verified, not corpus-verified. No part of any count change in this
wave may be credited to them. (This also makes L1's blast radius on reported numbers provably zero.)

**L3 — the pixel diff silently crops to the narrower capture, which is exactly where A9 lives.**
`gatePixels()` (`pixel-gate.ts:164-165`) computes over `min(source.width, clone.width) ×
min(source.height, clone.height)`, and captures are `fullPage: true`, so a PNG is as wide as the
document's scroll box:

| pair | source PNG | clone PNG | diffed at |
|---|---|---|---|
| linear `/` @700 | 700 × 9587 | **862** × 9960 | 700 × 9587 |
| severance index @1100 | **1280** × 2700 | 1100 × 2700 | 1100 × 2700 |

The horizontal overflow band is measured by `horizontal-overflow-excess-px` and
`offscreen-text-excess-chars` but is invisible to every pixel channel *and to the human review pack*,
where a reader is shown a 700px source beside an 862px clone without being told. The independent
visual auditor hit this and correctly refused to trust the pixel numbers on those rows.

**Neither L1 nor L3 is fixed.** Fixing L1 means making `overlap-excess-ratio-undemoted` fire, which is a
rubric change, which invalidates every self-check floor and every verdict measured in this wave. That
trade was not worth making at close-out; it is the first item of the 28.8 contract.

Programs 26 and 27 were **conditional** on fresh evidence. The gate was run fresh, and met.

Deciding measurement — linear `/`, fresh observation, source vs clone:

|---|---|---|
| 390 | 969 / 3,366 / 93 / 5,876 | 1,008 / 3,571 / 96 / 5,876 |

| 1440 | 3,094 / 9,205 / 249 / 9,960 | 3,060 / 8,760 / 249 / 9,960 |

The clone's `scrollHeight` is byte-identical at 390/700/1024 and again at 1100/1440 — two states, no
continuum — while the source varies continuously. `/` changes DOM family between 390 and 700;
`/pricing` between 1024 and 1100. **No single site-wide breakpoint can serve both.**

**§26 implemented** — per-route tree switch. `tree-switch.ts` gained a `byPage` map and
`decidePageTreeSwitch`; `app-template.ts` writes scoped media pairs, the site-wide pair excluding
overridden routes via `[data-wr-viewport="desktop"]:not([data-wr-page="pNNNNNN"])`.

**§27 implemented** — the working signal is **not** node population. My own hypothesis was wrong and an
agent corrected it with measurement: `totalNodes` is flat (4,704 on `/`) because linear ships both
variants and swaps them with `display`; a population-only fingerprint classifies linear as
CSS-responsive and changes nothing — **proved by mutation (2 checks red)**. The signal that works is
`getClientRects().length > 0` rendered-box counts plus a depth-6 FNV-1a structure hash. Per-route
results: `/` changes at 641 and 929; `/pricing` at 1025 only; `/changelog` nowhere, which is the
correct answer for a genuinely CSS-responsive route.

**Measured effect on linear `/`:** clone visible nodes 700 `1001→2511`, 1024 `991→2784`; `scrollHeight`
`5876→9960` at both. Every tree-selection channel — `image-presence-ratio`, `visible-text-ratio`,
`nav-link-ratio`, `scroll-height-ratio-low` — disappeared from `/`@700 and `/`@1024.
`missing-text-ratio` fell 0.4915→0.1071 at 700 and 0.5425→0.0774 at 1024.

**What it could not do.** Only two DOM trees are ever observed. Per-route switching moves *where* the
swap happens; it cannot invent a third variant. `/pricing`@1024 remains a BLOCKER
(`nav-link-ratio 0.500`) because its switch is at 1025 and no observed tree covers 1024 correctly.
The implementation records `variant-tree-not-observed-at-<width>` rather than silently serving the 390
tree. The mobile envelope extension is driven **only** by source-authored media conditions, capped at
2 brackets — 28.6 rejected raising the constant on foreknowledge and this wave kept that judgement.

## 18. Ideas Adopted

**`inset-resolved-width`** — the first population only, adopted unconditionally (flag removed).

`containingBlockGuard()` correctly refuses every existing inline-size branch for an out-of-flow box:
the parent's content box is not its containing block, so every measurement the funnel takes about the
parent is meaningless for it. Such a box reaches the end of the funnel with no rule and ships
Chromium's 1440-serialized px at every width. On linear.app that is the `position: fixed` site header
(`n000009`, `width: 1440px`, 49 descendants, on all 8 page × viewport passes). The new kind resolves
the width from the two definite inline insets instead — a different question, not a loosened guard.

**Measured:** **384 nodes improved, 2 worsened by 10px.** All 62 emitted rules survived the truth check.
hobbang.net output byte-identical. Adopted.
